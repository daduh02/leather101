Hide anatomy

Leather Cuts Explained

Different parts of the hide behave differently.

Back / Bend

Strongest and most consistent. Best for belts and straps.

Shoulder

Firm and versatile. Great for wallets, tooling and beginner projects.

Belly

Stretchier and less consistent. Good for practice and prototypes.

Side

Half of a hide with a mix of areas for larger projects.

Double Shoulder

Popular for craftwork and good value.

Full Hide

Best value for volume work but harder to store.

Hand sewing

Basic Stitching Guide

The classic leathercraft stitch is the saddle stitch.

1

Mark your stitch line

Keep the line consistent.

2

Punch the holes

Use stitching chisels and keep them vertical.

3

Thread two needles

Use one needle on each end of waxed thread.

4

Saddle stitch

Pass each needle through the same hole from opposite sides.

5

Backstitch

Go back two or three holes to finish.

Finishing

Edge Finishing Guide

Finished edges make projects look professional.

1. Trim

Make all layers flush.

2. Bevel

Remove sharp corners.

3. Sand

Sand the edge smooth.

4. Wet or gum

Use water, gum tragacanth or tokonole.

5. Burnish

Rub until smooth and glossy.

6. Seal

Use wax or edge finish.

Beginner Buying Advice

  1. Start with 3–4 oz vegetable-tanned full grain leather for small projects.
  2. Avoid bonded leather if you want something long lasting.
  3. Use chrome-tanned leather for softer goods like pouches and bags.
  4. Use shoulder or double shoulder leather for wallets, tooling and practice.
