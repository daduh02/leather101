Project sizing

Leather Thickness Guide

Leather thickness is often shown in ounces. As a simple rule, 1 oz is roughly 0.4 mm.

ThicknessApprox. mmBest ForBeginner Note
1–2 oz0.4–0.8 mmLining, small details, light foldsVery flexible but not strong on its own
2–3 oz0.8–1.2 mmWallet pockets, card slots, small pouchesGood for layered wallet interiors
3–4 oz1.2–1.6 mmWallet backs, notebook covers, small goodsBest beginner range for small projects
4–5 oz1.6–2.0 mmMedium pouches, soft cases, strapsStill easy enough to cut and stitch
5–7 oz2.0–2.8 mmBelts, dog collars, sheaths, structured goodsNeeds sharper tools and stronger stitching
8 oz+3.2 mm+Heavy belts, saddlery, armour, heavy-duty goodsNot ideal as a first leathercraft project

Hide anatomy

Leather Cuts Explained

Different parts of the hide behave differently. Pick the cut based on the project.

Back / Bend

The strongest and most consistent area. Best for belts, straps and premium goods.

Shoulder

Firm, characterful and versatile. Great for wallets, carving, tooling and beginner projects.

Belly

Stretchier and less consistent. Good for practice, prototypes and small flexible parts.

Side

Half of a hide. Offers a mix of shoulder, belly and back areas for larger projects.

Double Shoulder

Popular for craftwork because it gives usable size without buying a full hide.

Full Hide

Best value for volume work, but more expensive and harder to store.

Hand sewing

Basic Stitching Guide

The classic leathercraft stitch is the saddle stitch. It is strong, clean and beginner friendly.

1

Mark your stitch line

Use a ruler or wing divider. Keep the line consistent.

2

Punch the holes

Use stitching chisels and keep them vertical.

3

Thread two needles

Use one needle on each end of waxed thread.

4

Saddle stitch

Pass each needle through the same hole from opposite sides.

5

Backstitch to finish

Go back two or three holes, trim and secure the thread.

Finishing

Edge Finishing Guide

Finished edges make beginner projects look much more professional.

1. Trim

Make all layers flush.

2. Bevel

Remove sharp corners from both sides.

3. Sand

Sand the edge smooth.

4. Wet or gum

Use water, gum tragacanth or tokonole.

5. Burnish

Rub until smooth and glossy.

6. Seal

Use wax or edge finish for protection.

Beginner Buying Advice

  1. Start with 3–4 oz vegetable-tanned full grain leather for small craft projects.
  2. Avoid bonded leather if you want something long lasting.
  3. Use chrome-tanned leather for softer goods like pouches and bags.
  4. Use shoulder or double shoulder leather for wallets, tooling and practice.
