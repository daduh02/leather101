Beginner leather guide

Leather101

A simple guide to leather types, tanning methods, common uses and price levels.

Quick buying rule

Bonded is cheapest, genuine is basic, top grain is practical premium, full grain is best overall, and shell cordovan is luxury tier.

Ordered roughly from cheapest to most premium.

Tanning Types

Tanning is the process that turns raw hide into usable leather.

Tanning Type Process Best For Pros Cons Cost
Chrome Tanned Uses chromium salts Bags, upholstery, fashion Soft, flexible, fast to produce Less natural ageing ££
Vegetable Tanned Uses plant tannins from bark, leaves and roots Belts, wallets, tooling Ages well, develops patina, firm structure Stiffer and slower to produce ££££
Combination Tanned Uses both chrome and vegetable tanning methods Boots and mixed-use goods Balanced softness and durability Less traditional character £££
Aldehyde Tanned Uses non-chrome chemical tanning Car interiors and light-coloured leather Soft and suitable for pale colours Less character than veg tan £££
Oil Tanned Finished with oils and waxes Work boots and rugged bags Flexible and more water resistant Less formal finish £££

Beginner Buying Advice

  1. Start with vegetable-tanned full grain leather for craft projects.
  2. Avoid bonded leather if you want something long lasting.
  3. Use chrome-tanned leather for softer goods like pouches and bags.
  4. Use full grain veg tan for premium wallets, belts and tooling.
